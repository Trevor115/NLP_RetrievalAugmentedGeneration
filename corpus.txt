Retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG, combines document retrieval with language generation. A system first retrieves relevant documents and then uses them to produce a grounded answer.

TF-IDF stands for term frequency-inverse document frequency. It helps identify which words are important in a document relative to a larger collection.

Language models sometimes hallucinate by producing answers that sound reasonable but are not supported by facts. This happens because they rely on learned patterns rather than verified sources.

Dense retrieval uses embeddings instead of word-count vectors. It can help address vocabulary mismatch by finding semantically similar documents.

Cosine similarity measures how similar two vectors are. In information retrieval, it is commonly used to compare a query vector with document vectors.
